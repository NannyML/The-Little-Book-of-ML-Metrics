## Direct preferences and repeated feedback

### Natural, human language

Source: September 15, 2026, "Improve Landing Page Copy."

Apply: Prefer ordinary phrasing, direct explanations, and first-read comprehension over polished marketing language.

### Simplicity and brevity

Source: September 18, 2026, "Simplify Product Update."

Apply: Remove repetition and complicated syntax. Preserve the facts that make the update useful.

Apply: Treat grammar checks as light edits, not permission to replace the original voice or framing.

### Reader value in product communication

Source: August 31, 2026, "Improve email copy."

> always focus on value for the user

Later requests tied a feature to conversations moving with the user between pages and to improving data quality faster.

Apply: Explain what a feature lets the reader do, rather than merely announcing the feature.

### Self-contained questions

Source: September 18, 2026, "Design onboarding form questions."

> they should be a bit more self contained to understand them easier

Apply: Make a form question understandable without mentally reconstructing the previous screen. Review question order, choice order, and wording as part of the flow.

### Shorter flows, not just shorter sentences

Source: September 18, 2026, "Design onboarding form questions."

> I would maybe still remove a couple of questions to make it even shorter, dont you think?

Apply: Consider removing steps that do not affect the decision or user outcome. Do not assume every proposed question must stay.

### Preserve useful keywords

Source: September 15, 2026, "Improve Landing Page Copy."

> we want to rank for keywords like: cron job, edge function

Apply: Keep explicit keyword requirements naturally within the relevant campaign. This is not a requirement to mention those terms in unrelated writing.

### Preserve a valued part of the message

Source: September 15, 2026, "Improve Landing Page Copy."

> I liked that the previous one had something about fix it from slack

Apply: When revising, retain parts the user specifically values. Do not independently assert that Slack can perform a fix; confirm the actual capability in the current brief.

### Explain technical subjects plainly

Source: September 14, 2026, "Rewrite Flyer Copy," and September 17 follow-up.

> in my own voice from it explaining easily what a data contract is

Apply: Explain the concept plainly and use a concrete example before adding complexity. Follow the requested format and duration for scripts.

## Reasonable defaults inferred from repeated interactions

Use these as revisable defaults, not claims that Santiago explicitly dictated them:

- Preserve first-person observations and curiosity in personal posts.
- Give the edited text directly for ordinary grammar requests.
- Offer one strong version unless options are requested.
- Prefer concrete situations to vague category language when addressing builders.
- Keep CTAs ordinary, specific, and low-friction.
- Preserve the author's tone and meaning rather than maximizing formality.

## Keep local requests local

"Dont start with takes about a minute" referred to one onboarding form introduction. It supports leading with purpose there, not a universal prohibition on duration estimates.

"not sure if the word catch is bad" expressed uncertainty about a Reddit title. It does not establish a ban on "catch."

A request to omit a video reference, avoid a word in one sentence, or mention Slack belongs to its own draft unless repeated as a standing instruction.

The supplied Sodabase positioning favored the vocabulary of Supabase builders and avoided abstract data-industry labels for that audience. Do not apply those exclusions to all Soda marketing, documentation, or technical writing.

## Do not promote unverified interpretations into rules

The available user feedback does not establish a blanket ban on em dashes, symmetrical bullets, "not X but Y" constructions, or specific individual words.

It also does not establish a preference for incorrect grammar, deliberate typos, or broken English. Preserve the voice while correcting errors.

Evidence is strongest for short-form English work communication, product copy, community posts, forms, and scripts. Apply the same clarity principles to other formats without pretending that long-form, literary, or multilingual style has been fully characterized.

## Update conservatively

When new feedback arrives, classify it as an explicit standing preference, a repeated pattern, or a task-specific exception. Update the narrowest applicable rule. Do not turn one rejected sentence into a universal lexical ban or silently treat an assistant-proposed example as user-approved writing.

## Connected prose: explicit standing preference, September 21, 2026

During the Data Observability review, Santiago said the clearer revision still felt “too stiff” and rejected both “fluffy ai writing” and sentences that sound “robotic and souless.” He explicitly asked to encode this preference in the writing skills and memory.

Apply: preserve clarity and precision, but let sentences connect and the explanation develop. Concision is not a target for the shortest possible sentences. Natural rhythm should come from phrasing and logical connections, not added stories or personality. His “exactly” endorsed this direction and the preceding sample revisions; those examples are calibration, not mandatory wording.
