\begin{bookreadingguide}
\section{Who this is for}

We wrote this book for data scientists, ML engineers and anyone working with machine learning models. You should be familiar with confusion matrices and residuals; we explain the metrics as we go.

\section{How to read a metric page}

Most metrics have a two-page spread. On the left, we explain what the metric measures, how to read its formula and score, and when to use it. Strengths and weaknesses sit side by side.

On the right, a figure and its explanation show the metric at work. A ``Did you know'' note adds context, and related metrics point to other ways of evaluating the same task.

\section{Formula colors}

In the annotated formulas, color shows the role of each term.

\vspace{4pt}
\guidecolor{nmlcyan}{Cyan}{Observed values: targets and labels.}
\guidecolor{nmlpurple}{Purple}{Model outputs: predictions, scores and estimates.}
\guidecolor{nmlred}{Red}{Counts and sizes: samples, classes and bins.}
\guidecolor{nmlgreen}{Green}{Functions, such as the indicator $\mathds{1}[\cdot]$.}
\guidecolor{nmlyellow}{Yellow}{Settings: thresholds, weights and quantile levels.}

\section{How the book is organized}

Start with the chapter for your task, then use its decision map to find a metric. The final three subject chapters cover performance estimation, fairness and data observability.
