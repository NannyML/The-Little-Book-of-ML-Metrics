\begin{bookreadingguide}
\section{Who this is for}

We wrote this book for data scientists, ML and AI engineers, and anyone who wants to understand what goes into evaluating ML/AI/SI models. We assume basic ML knowledge; it helps if you're familiar with residuals, confusion matrices, and targets versus predictions.

\section{How to read a metric page}

Most metrics have a two-page spread. On the left, we explain what the metric measures, how to read its formula and score, and where it works well or falls short. On the right, we show it in action with example data, share a bit of history or an interesting property, and compare it with related metrics.

\section{Formula colors}

Almost every metric in this book has a formula. We've annotated the formulas to make them easier to follow and tried to keep the colors consistent. Here's the logic behind them.

\vspace{4pt}
\guidecolor{nmlcyan}{Cyan}{Observed values: targets and labels.}
\guidecolor{nmlpurple}{Purple}{Model outputs: predictions, scores and estimates.}
\guidecolor{nmlred}{Red}{Counts and sizes: samples, classes and bins.}
\guidecolor{nmlgreen}{Green}{Functions, such as the indicator $\mathds{1}[\cdot]$.}
\guidecolor{nmlyellow}{Yellow}{Parameters: thresholds, weights and quantile levels.}

\section{How the book is organized}

In this little, not so little book, we've grouped metrics into ten chapters: Regression, Classification, Clustering, Ranking, Computer Vision, NLP, GenAI, Probabilistic, Bias \& Fairness, and Data Observability. Each chapter opens with a decision tree to help you pick metrics for your task, and when applicable finishes with a diagram explaining how to translate metrics from one to the other.
